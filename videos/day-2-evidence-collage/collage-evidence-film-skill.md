## Mandatory asset-sheet and depth-map pass
Before building a frame, produce a small asset sheet and a layer map. Every held phase has these responsibilities:
1. **Background texture:** quiet paper, print grid, or a crop strip. It never contains functional text.
2. **Factual primary:** one readable screenshot, source strip, or code/action artefact. It alone may carry claims.
3. **Physical support:** one or two non-competing pieces—tape, a torn edge, archive tab, marker circle, receipt, or paper fold—that explain why the primary belongs in the story.
4. **Foreground depth:** an optional crop mask, paper edge, or illustrative `forger pin` that moves independently. It may create a sandwich/occlusion relationship, but can never cover readable text or factual image detail.

The `forger pin` is a deliberately illustrative paper token, not a user, customer, product metric, or factual evidence. It can guide the eye source → code → new fit, then leave before a new reading object arrives.

## Motion grammar
- **Build layers, not cards.** Establish the paper field, then reveal the factual primary, then add the support or foreground layer. Do not reveal a complete infographic all at once.
- **Use controlled independent motion.** A background strip may drift 12–24px, a source folio may settle, and the forger pin may cross one clear gutter. Each motion has a different duration/ease and stops before reading begins.
- **Use sandwich depth deliberately.** Put an illustrative foreground edge in front of a factual crop only at a non-text edge; let the primary crop visibly sit between background paper and foreground material.
- **Posterize selectively.** The stamp, pin, marker, or one source tab may move in two or three stepped holds. Keep captions, primary screenshots, and code smooth and readable.
- **Object actions need audio intent.** Paper entrance, marker draw, stamp land, and receipt resolve may later receive quiet foley. Do not add synthetic foley until the visual action is locked.

## Mobile composition rules
1. **One active evidence object per spoken clause.** A screenshot, source clipping, or code excerpt dominates; prior evidence becomes a thin tab or exits.
2. **Protect the reading field.** Functional content stays within `x:72–1008`, `y:120–1430`. Captions begin at `y:1600`. Maximum one 2–6 word display statement plus one small provenance label per held phase.
3. **Routes use gutters, never copy.** A coral route enters/exits at object edges, crosses only empty paper, and disappears before the next reading object arrives.
4. **Preserve source integrity.** Captured screenshots are cropable evidence, never faint generic backgrounds. Obstructed or unreadable captures become truthful title/domain strips only.
5. **Captions support, never duplicate.** Suppress them for the final CTA and during small factual source reads.

## Brand and anti-patterns
Use fondof cream `#F7F1E5`, ink `#221812`, coral `#C5521C`, and warm navy `#181615` for code. Keep Outfit for readable display/body and Geist Mono for source labels/code. Avoid repeated rounded application cards, generic `CITATION` / `EVIDENCE` labels, placeholder URLs, fake browser UI, invented quotes, invented metrics, or generated people.

Replicate cutouts are permitted only after a user provides a key and approves a source-specific asset sheet. Keep them clearly illustrative and text-free; they augment non-evidence collage layers and never substitute for factual source/product evidence.
